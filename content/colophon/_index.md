A colophon is the note at the back of a book that tells you how it was made: the typeface, the paper, the printer, sometimes a little prayer. I've always read them first.

**The site itself** is set in three faces. Headlines are in [Fraunces](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Fraunces), a slightly wonky "Old Style" revival drawn by Phaedra Charles and Flavia Zimbardi at Undercase Type. Body text is in [Newsreader](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Newsreader) by Production Type, made for reading long things on screens. Labels are in [IBM Plex Mono](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/IBM+Plex+Mono) by Mike Abbink and Bold Monday. The paper is warm, the ink is nearly black, and there's one red, the colour printers have used for the important bits since the rubricators of the 15th century.

**The essays** each borrow from one designer, one place and one moment. The book that started this for me is Robert Bringhurst's *The Elements of Typographic Style*, which I'm not ashamed to say is about as close to a bible as I've got. Bringhurst writes that typography exists to honour content. I took that seriously and then, I admit, a little too far.

These are tributes, not reproductions. Most of the original typefaces are expensive, extinct, or both, so each page uses the nearest honest substitute from the open-source shelf, and says so. If you know these people better than I do, and some of you will, write to me and tell me what I got wrong.

The site is built with Hugo, hosted on Netlify, and loads no trackers. It has no comments section, on purpose. If something here moves you, [write me a letter](mailto:ganis@atmawarin.com).
