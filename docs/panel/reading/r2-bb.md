## 2. Top 3
1. **Option 2, Date Due.** Biggest wow per risk. The stamp is tactile, funny, and says "reader, not brand." No scroll dependency.
2. **Option 9, Rubricator's Pass.** Best answer to the category problem, and a single 700ms red line is a screenshot moment.
3. **Option 6, Reading Year.** Shows the range and the 2023 gap as a shape. It is the most honest object on the page.

## 3. What I'd ship
**Primary: Option 2 (Date Due) as the page. Supporting layer: Option 9's red pen as the starred toggle**, replacing the chips. Borrow Option 6's year jump links as the nav, no rail animation.

First 10 seconds: the page opens on a typed library card, year 2025, a calm list. The first screen mixes a dad book, a novel, a typography book. The visitor moves the cursor down a row and a red date stamp thunks onto it, slightly crooked. Starred books already carry AGAIN stamps. One toggle reads "Show what I read again." Click it and a red line draws down the margin, the 80 others dim, and 24 books are underlined.

The send-to-a-friend moment: **hovering Bringhurst and watching five AGAIN stamps pile up**, then the 2023 card stamped NIL. Nobody else's reading page does that.

## 4. Rulings
- **Scroll-triggered arrival motion: NO.** I withdraw my r1 "ink-in once." The page's two moments are caused by the visitor (hover/tap stamp, toggle pen). Reduced motion and no-JS get static stamps.
- **Covers: summoned, not visible.** Slide out of the card pocket on hover/focus/tap. A cover wall is the business-book billboard and the quality is inconsistent.
- **Categories: remove the chips; one starred toggle.** No genre taxonomy shown. If subject headings return later, alphabetical, never ranked by count.

## 5. Debate
- **Against PM (arrival stamp):** I held that position and drop it. Stamping on arrival is a load-time entrance in disguise, and it would fire on a page people open from a link, before they've decided to play. The stamp is better as a reward for touching.
- **Against SD/IxD/PM (covers visible as small plates):** a visible plate strip puts 40 business covers in the first screen. That is exactly the hustle read. Summoned covers keep range in the visitor's hands.
- **Against IxD (fix the chips):** a fixed chip row still invites sorting by genre, and "Business & Leadership" would be the biggest chip. One neutral toggle does the job.
- **Concession to V:** I preferred Reading Year as the lead. V's argument that the page needs a *physical gesture with a sense of humour*, not a chart, changed my mind. A rail is a dashboard until you touch it. Stamps are personality.

## 6. Option 10, Leaning Shelf
**Verdict: a gimmick for this site, though a real wow.** It wows the first time, then it's a toy; fling-physics 104 spines reads "look what I built," which is the agency-demo tell. It fails Fit because the site is a specimen book, quiet and printed. It also has the worst phone and battery story, and sway on a reading page is performance, not reading.

It becomes acceptable if it shrinks to **one gesture**: pulling a spine makes the two neighbours lean 3° into the gap and settle in 300ms. That is a detail inside Option 1, not an engine. No fling, no velocity, no weight-by-star. Starred books can simply carry the red.

## 7. Blockers (hard no from my lane)
- Any genre-count display, chip, or sort that makes "Business & Leadership" (40 of 104) the loudest label.
- Reading-goal, streak, or "N books this year" styled as achievement. Count stays small mono.
- Invented notes, reasons or ratings for the 24 starred. Option 9's "why" notes are Ganis's words or nothing.
- Any share buttons, Goodreads/Amazon links, recent-opened memory. Local covers only.
- Option 8 leading with a quote Ganis hasn't flagged as his pick; first-visible quote must not be a business one by default.
- Option 10 as specified (fling, per-star weight): fidget toy, and "heavier = more important" is a ranking.
- Date Due, Option 2: placeholder dates must not show as real days. Month-year only until Ganis confirms.
