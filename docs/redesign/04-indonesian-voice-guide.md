# Indonesian voice guide: "Ganis in Bahasa"

Ganis feels his Indonesian essays read as a mix of styles. His English essays have one voice: David Sedaris-style, specific, deadpan, self-deprecating, and ending on a quiet emotional turn. The Indonesian essays need one voice too, built from Indonesian traditions of witty personal essays.

## Lineage (emulate the spirit; never copy their text)
- **Umar Kayam**, the *Mangan Ora Mangan Kumpul* columns in *Kedaulatan Rakyat*, Yogyakarta. Take from him gentle Javanese irony, warmth towards ordinary people, a narrator who is slightly ridiculous and knows it, and observation over preaching.
- **The Mojok / Puthut EA school** (also Yogya). Take from it self-mockery, confessional asides, the reader treated as a friend at an *angkringan*, and humour that comes from honesty.
- **Ganis himself.** He code-switches. An English phrase lands where it naturally would ("The thing is", "deadline", "ship", "honestly"). Keep that, but never more than one English phrase per paragraph or so.

## Rules
1. **Narrator.** Write in the first person with *saya* (keep *saya* where the essay uses it). The narrator is never the hero. He is the one who got it slightly wrong first.
2. **Show a small scene before any point.** Use only the scenes, people, places and numbers already in the essay. **Do not invent anecdotes, quotes, people or facts.** If the essay has no scene, sharpen the observation instead of fabricating one.
3. **Cut the tells of the motivational listicle:**
   - generic lessons ("Pelajarannya adalah...", "Intinya...", "Pada akhirnya...")
   - rhetorical questions to the reader that the next line answers
   - "bukan hanya X, tapi juga Y"
   - stacked aphorisms
   - closing paragraphs that summarise
4. **End quietly.** Finish on a concrete image or a small admission, not a moral. One short sentence is fine.
5. **Rhythm.** Mostly short sentences, with a longer one when the thought needs it. Single-line paragraphs only when they earn a laugh or a pause.
6. **Keep the structure.**
   - Keep the essay's headings (in sentence case), lists, images and shortcodes, quotes from other people (verbatim) and links.
   - List essays (Principle, Hal Terbaik di 2018) stay lists. Change the voice of each item, not the list.
7. **Punctuation.** No em dashes and no en dashes as dashes; use a period or a comma. Use straight quotes. The site's typographer turns them curly.
8. **Front matter.** Don't touch the front matter, except that you may rewrite the `description:` and `dek:` values in the same voice. Keep the `dek` to 100 characters or fewer, and keep both in double quotes.
9. **Length.** Stay within ±15% of the current word count.
10. **Respect the date.** The essay keeps its date. A 2018 essay was written by a 2018 Ganis, so don't add hindsight.
11. **Self-audit before you finish.**
    - Read it aloud in your head as if Ganis were telling it at an angkringan at 10 pm. Fix every line that sounds like a seminar slide.
    - Then check for AI tells: "Di era digital ini", "tak dapat dipungkiri", "sangat penting untuk", "dengan demikian", "merupakan", "menjadi kunci".
